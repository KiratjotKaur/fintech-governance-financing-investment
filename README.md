 Corporate Governance, Financing Choices, and Investment in Indian FinTech Firms

About This Project

This is an independent corporate finance research project examining the relationship between corporate governance, financing choices, and visible investment patterns among Indian listed FinTech-linked firms.

I developed this project to gain practical experience in financial research, including data collection from public disclosures, construction of financial and governance variables, dataset preparation, descriptive analysis, and interpretation of firm-level relationships.

## Research Questions

The project examines three questions:

1. How are financing choices associated with corporate investment?
2. How are governance characteristics, particularly board independence, associated with investment decisions?
3. What patterns emerge when governance characteristics, financing choices, and investment are examined together?

## Dataset

The dataset consists of:

- 25 Indian listed FinTech-linked firms
- FY2020-21 to FY2023-24
- 100 firm-year observations
- Hand-collected financial and corporate governance data
- Data obtained primarily from company annual reports and corporate governance disclosures

The sample includes firms operating across digital payments, digital broking and wealth-tech, FinTech infrastructure, BFSI software, digital lending/NBFCs, insurance intermediation, and related financial services.

## Variables

Five main variables are examined:

- Investment = Capital Expenditure / Total Assets
- Leverage = Total Debt / Total Assets
- ROA = Net Income / Total Assets
- Firm Size = Natural Logarithm of Total Assets
- Board Independence = Independent Directors / Board Size

## Methodology

The analysis uses:

- Descriptive statistics
- Year-on-year trend analysis
- Pearson correlation analysis

The project is descriptive and exploratory. Correlations are interpreted as associations and not as evidence of causality.

## Key Findings

The analysis identifies several patterns within the sample:

- Leverage and investment show a moderate negative association (r = -0.380).
- Firm size and investment show a negative association (r = -0.277).
- Board independence has only a weak positive association with investment (r = 0.078).
- Firm size and board independence are positively associated (r = 0.400).
- Mean firm size and board independence increased across the observation period, while investment and leverage remained comparatively stable.

These findings should be interpreted within the boundaries of the selected sample and methodology.

## Limitations

This project uses a relatively small, purposively selected sample and a four-year observation period. It relies on publicly available company disclosures and uses descriptive and correlation analysis rather than causal econometric methods.

## Future Research

Future work could extend this project by:

- Expanding the number of firms and observation years
- Incorporating ownership structure and additional governance variables
- Comparing Indian firms with firms in other emerging economies, particularly China
- Examining corporate tax behaviour and risk-taking
- Applying panel-data and multivariate methods to a larger dataset
